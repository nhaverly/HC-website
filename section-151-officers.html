The role at a glance

Section 151 of the Local Government Act 1972 requires every council to make arrangements for the proper administration of its financial affairs, and to make one officer responsible for them. That officer, usually the director of finance, is known as the section 151 officer or chief finance officer.

Three statutory duties define the role in practice:

  • Section 114 reports. Under the Local Government Finance Act 1988, the chief finance officer must report if the council has made, or is about to make, unlawful expenditure, or if expenditure is likely to exceed the resources available to meet it.
  • Section 25 reports. Under the Local Government Act 2003, the chief finance officer must report to council, when it sets the budget, on the robustness of the estimates and the adequacy of reserves.
  • Prudential borrowing. The council sets its own borrowing limits under the CIPFA Prudential Code, and the chief finance officer advises on whether capital plans are affordable, prudent and sustainable.

CIPFA's statement on the role of the chief financial officer in local government sets out the principles behind the role, including direct access to the chief executive and a seat at the leadership table.

This page focuses on England. Sections 151 and 114 also apply in Wales, where councils follow Welsh Government guidance. Scotland and Northern Ireland have their own frameworks.

The annual cycle

The key statutory and regulatory dates in a typical English council's financial year.

  • 1 AprilNew financial year begins. New social rents take effect, within the limit set by the Rent Standard. Source: Rent Standard and guidance
  • QuarterlyReport treasury management activity and prudential indicators. Quarters 2 and 4 normally go to full council or the executive. Source: CIPFA Prudential Code
  • By 30 JunePublish the draft (unaudited) statement of accounts and annual governance statement. Source: Accounts and Audit Regulations 2015
  • Summer to autumnReport the annual treasury management review of the previous year to members. Source: CIPFA Treasury Management Code
  • AutumnUK Budget. Refresh the medium-term financial strategy and test the assumptions behind next year's budget.
  • DecemberProvisional local government finance settlement, usually published in mid to late December. Source: Local government finance settlement
  • 1 December to 31 JanuaryBilling authorities calculate the council tax base and notify precepting authorities. Source: Council tax base regulations
  • January to FebruarySet rents and the HRA budget for April, update the HRA 30-year business plan and give tenants notice of rent changes. Source: Policy statement on rents
  • FebruaryFinal settlement. Full council approves the budget, capital strategy, treasury management strategy and MRP statement, with the chief finance officer's section 25 report on the robustness of estimates and adequacy of reserves. Source: Local Government Act 2003, s25
  • Before 1 MarchMajor precepting authorities issue their precepts. Source: Local Government Finance Act 1992, s40
  • Before 11 MarchBilling authorities set council tax for the coming year. Source: Local Government Finance Act 1992, s30
  • 1 to 31 MarchSubmit capital spending and borrowing plans for the current year and next two years to the PWLB, with the section 151 officer's assurance on investment assets bought primarily for yield. Source: PWLB guidance for applicants

  • CIPFA: The role of the chief financial officer in local governmentCIPFA's statement of the principles and responsibilities of the role.

  • PWLB guidance for applicantsEligibility, the annual capital plan return and the section 151 officer's assurance.
